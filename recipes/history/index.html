    Market History with BDH¶

    BQL screens a universe and computes. BDH, bq.bdh() here and =BDH() in Excel, does something simpler: it returns the history of a few fields for a list of securities. The query is short, so the work is in the options.

    The options decide which dates you get (days, Fridays, month-ends, fiscal years) and what happens on a day when one market is closed and another is open. Get them wrong and you get a frame that looks right but has gaps, half-empty rows or misaligned dates.

    Data on this page

    This page uses the series a credit desk pulls every week: bond index spreads, CDS indices, fund NAVs and flows, the cost of hedging dollars into euros, government yields, fiscal-year fundamentals and price candles.

    The outputs below are live data as of 2026-11-17 and will differ when you run the queries. The recipes with fixed dates change only when Bloomberg revises its data. Before you adapt a query, read the Pitfalls.

    Quick reference¶

    bdh() returns a long frame with a security column, a date column and one column per field. pivot(on="security", ...) makes one column per security.

    You want Use One row per
    Weekly, monthly or yearly closes options={"periodicitySelection": "WEEKLY"}, MONTHLY, YEARLY security and period
    Markets with different holidays on the same dates "nonTradingDayFillOption": "NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS", "nonTradingDayFillMethod": "PREVIOUS_VALUE" security and weekday
    A fund's NAV, assets and flows FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL, FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS, FUND_CLASS_ASSETS, FUND_FLOW fund and date
    A CDS index against its constituents CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_VALUE, CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_BASIS_VALUE index and date
    FX, hedging cost and yields side by side several securities, PX_LAST, then pivot(on="security") date
    Fundamentals per fiscal year "periodicitySelection": "YEARLY", "periodicityAdjustment": "FISCAL" company and fiscal year
    Values in another currency "currency": "USD"
    Open, high, low and close PX_OPEN, PX_HIGH, PX_LOW, PX_LAST security and day, week or month

    The options go in one dict, options={...}. The values are strings.

    Option Values Default Effect
    periodicitySelection DAILY, WEEKLY, MONTHLY, QUARTERLY, SEMI_ANNUALLY, YEARLY DAILY One row per period
    periodicityAdjustment CALENDAR, ACTUAL, FISCAL calendar periods CALENDAR: weeks end on Friday, months on the last business day. ACTUAL: periods end on end_date, so weeks end on its weekday. FISCAL: on each company's fiscal period end
    nonTradingDayFillOption ACTIVE_DAYS_ONLY, NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS, ALL_CALENDAR_DAYS ACTIVE_DAYS_ONLY Which dates get a row
    nonTradingDayFillMethod PREVIOUS_VALUE, NIL_VALUE PREVIOUS_VALUE What an added date holds
    currency EUR, USD, SEK, ... the security's own Converts prices, fund assets and fundamentals

    start_date and end_date take a date or a string, "2026-04-01" or "20260401".

    High-yield spreads, week by week¶

    periodicitySelection=WEEKLY, INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP

    Friday OAS of four Bloomberg bond indices for the last twelve weeks: US High Yield (LF98TRUU), its Industrials sector (I00423), Pan-European High Yield (LP01TREU) and Contingent Capital EUR (I30904EU). In basis points.

    from datetime import date, timedelta

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

indices = {
    "LF98TRUU Index": "us_hy",
    "I00423 Index": "us_hy_industrials",
    "LP01TREU Index": "euro_hy",
    "I30904EU Index": "eur_coco",
}
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        list(indices),
        ["INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP"],
        start_date=date.today() - timedelta(weeks=12),
        end_date=date.today(),
        options={"periodicitySelection": "WEEKLY"},
    )
oas = df.with_columns(pl.col("security").replace_strict(indices)).pivot(
    on="security", index="date", values="INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP"
)
with pl.Config(tbl_rows=20):
    print(oas.select("date", pl.col(list(indices.values())).round().cast(pl.Int64)))

    Watch out

    • WEEKLY gives each week's Friday close. The current week is not there until it is complete. For the latest close, add a daily request or use BQL's per=W, as in Index yield and spread, week by week.
    • "periodicityAdjustment": "ACTUAL" ends each week on the weekday of end_date. Run it on a Wednesday and you get Wednesday closes.

    Notes

    • INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP is the OAS in basis points, and INDEX_OAS_TSY is the same number in percent. INDEX_Z_SPREAD_BP is the Z-spread.
    • Field ids work as well as mnemonics: "BX218" is INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP and "BX326" is INDEX_Z_SPREAD_BP. The column is named as you asked, so BX218. Look fields up with FLDS <GO>.
    • replace_strict turns tickers into short names before the pivot. It raises an error for a ticker that is missing from the dict, so a typo does not go unnoticed.

    Three markets, one calendar¶

    nonTradingDayFillOption, nonTradingDayFillMethod

    The S&P 500, Euro Stoxx 50 and OMX Stockholm 30 around Easter 2026. On Good Friday, 3 April, all three were closed. On Easter Monday, 6 April, only New York traded. The recipe asks for every weekday, carries the last close into holidays and lists the markets that were closed.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

markets = ["SPX Index", "SX5E Index", "OMX Index"]
every_weekday = {
    "nonTradingDayFillOption": "NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS",
    "nonTradingDayFillMethod": "PREVIOUS_VALUE",
}
with BQuery() as bq:
    traded = bq.bdh(markets, "PX_LAST", "2026-04-01", "2026-04-08")
    filled = bq.bdh(
        markets, "PX_LAST", "2026-04-01", "2026-04-08", options=every_weekday
    )
closed = (
    filled.join(traded, on=["security", "date"], how="anti")
    .group_by("date")
    .agg(closed=pl.col("security").str.replace(" Index", "").sort().str.join(", "))
)
print(
    filled.pivot(on="security", index="date", values="PX_LAST")
    .join(closed, on="date", how="left")
    .sort("date")
    .select("date", pl.col("date").dt.strftime("%a").alias("day"), *markets, "closed")
)

    Watch out

    • Without options (traded) each security keeps its own trading days. Good Friday has no row at all, and Easter Monday has one only for the S&P 500. After a pivot Europe is null on Easter Monday. Computed per security, Europe's return on Tuesday covers Thursday to Tuesday, and New York's only Monday to Tuesday.
    • nonTradingDayFillMethod alone changes nothing. The default nonTradingDayFillOption, ACTIVE_DAYS_ONLY, adds no days to fill.

    Notes

    • NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS gives a row for every Monday to Friday. PREVIOUS_VALUE repeats the last close on the added days, so the return on a holiday is 0.
    • ALL_CALENDAR_DAYS adds Saturdays and Sundays too, which helps when a month ends on a weekend. NIL_VALUE adds the days but leaves them null.
    • The anti join finds the rows that only the fill created. Those are the holidays.

    Fund NAV, assets and flows¶

    FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS, FUND_FLOW, MONTHLY

    Month-end NAV, return and assets of two AT1 funds: the SEB Hybrid Capital Bond Fund (SEBVVGD, a UCITS fund, share class R EUR) and the Invesco USD AT1 CoCo Bond UCITS ETF (AT1 LN). class_flow is Bloomberg's flow into the share class. fund_flow estimates the flow into the whole fund from its assets and the NAV return. Assets and flows are in millions of the fund's currency, EUR and USD.

    from datetime import date, timedelta

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        ["SEBVVGD SS Equity", "AT1 LN Equity"],
        ["FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL", "FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS", "FUND_FLOW"],
        start_date=date.today() - timedelta(days=200),
        end_date=date.today(),
        options={"periodicitySelection": "MONTHLY"},
    )
growth = pl.col("FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL") / pl.col("FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL").shift()
aum = pl.col("FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS")
table = (
    df.with_columns(
        ret_pct=(100 * (growth - 1)).over("security"),
        fund_flow=(aum - aum.shift() * growth).over("security"),
    )
    .drop_nulls("fund_flow")
    .select(
        fund=pl.col("security").str.split(" ").list.first(),
        month="date",
        nav=pl.col("FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL").round(2),
        ret_pct=pl.col("ret_pct").round(2),
        aum=aum.round(1),
        class_flow=pl.col("FUND_FLOW").round(1),
        fund_flow=pl.col("fund_flow").round(1),
    )
)
with pl.Config(tbl_rows=20):
    print(table)

    Watch out

    • With MONTHLY, FUND_FLOW is the sum of the month's daily flows. NAV and assets are month-end values. The current month is missing until it ends.
    • PX_LAST of a mutual fund is its NAV. For an ETF it is the exchange price, and PX_LAST / FUND_NET_ASSET_VAL - 1 is the premium to NAV.

    Notes

    • FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS is the whole fund, all share classes together. FUND_CLASS_ASSETS is the share class of the ticker you ask for, and FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS_CRNCY gives the currency.
    • Assets are shares times NAV. So the change in assets less the NAV return is the money that came in or went out. Computed with FUND_CLASS_ASSETS, it comes close to Bloomberg's FUND_FLOW. With FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS it is an estimate for the whole fund, because the other share classes earn slightly different returns (other currencies, other fees).
    • A month where class_flow is close to 0 and fund_flow is large means money moved in the fund's other share classes.
    • "currency": "SEK" returns the assets in SEK. Bloomberg converts them.

    CDS index vs intrinsic: the basis¶

    CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_BASIS_VALUE

    iTraxx Europe Main and Crossover, 5-year, for the last three weeks. For each index the quoted spread, the intrinsic spread (the index spread implied by the CDS of its constituents) and the basis between them, in basis points.

    from datetime import date, timedelta

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

indices = {"ITRXEBE Index": "main", "ITRXEXE Index": "xover"}
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        list(indices),
        ["PX_LAST", "CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_VALUE", "CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_BASIS_VALUE"],
        start_date=date.today() - timedelta(weeks=3),
        end_date=date.today(),
    )
wide = df.select(
    "date",
    index=pl.col("security").replace_strict(indices),
    spread=pl.col("PX_LAST").round(1),
    intrinsic=pl.col("CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_VALUE").round(1),
    basis=pl.col("CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_BASIS_VALUE"),
).pivot(on="index", index="date", values=["spread", "intrinsic", "basis"])
with pl.Config(tbl_rows=30):
    print(
        wide.select(
            "date",
            "spread_main",
            "intrinsic_main",
            "basis_main",
            "spread_xover",
            "intrinsic_xover",
            "basis_xover",
        )
    )

    Watch out

    • basis is the quoted spread minus the intrinsic spread, although the field's description says it the other way round. A positive basis means the index trades wider than its constituents.
    • ITRXEBE and ITRXEXE follow the current series. They roll to a new series around 20 March and 20 September. The spread jumps on the roll, because the new series runs six months longer and some of its companies change. Main closed at 54.6 on Friday 18 September 2026 and at 58.2 on the roll day, Monday 21 September; Crossover went from 260 to 284.

    Notes

    • Today's quoted spread is there during the day, but the intrinsic spread and the basis are still null. drop_nulls("basis_main") removes that row.
    • values=[...] in pivot gives one column per field and index, named <field>_<index>.
    • For the return of selling protection on Main, use the excess return index ERIXITEU Index.

    US Treasuries hedged into euros vs Bunds¶

    FXHCEUUS Index, pivot(on="security")

    Should a euro investor buy 5-year US Treasuries hedged back into euros, or 5-year Bunds? Month-end EURUSD, the cost of hedging dollars into euros with 3-month forwards, both yields, and the hedged pickup over Bunds. Four securities, one field, one wide frame.

    from datetime import date, timedelta

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

names = {
    "EURUSD Curncy": "eurusd",
    "FXHCEUUS Index": "hedge_cost",
    "USGG5YR Index": "ust_5y",
    "GDBR5 Index": "bund_5y",
}
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        list(names),
        "PX_LAST",
        start_date=date.today() - timedelta(days=365),
        end_date=date.today(),
        options={"periodicitySelection": "MONTHLY"},
    )
wide = df.with_columns(pl.col("security").replace_strict(names)).pivot(
    on="security", index="date", values="PX_LAST"
)
hedged = pl.col("ust_5y") + pl.col("hedge_cost")
with pl.Config(tbl_rows=20):
    print(
        wide.select(
            "date",
            *names.values(),
            ust_hedged=hedged.round(2),
            pickup_bp=(100 * (hedged - pl.col("bund_5y"))).round(),
        )
    )

    Watch out

    FXHCEUUS (Bloomberg EURUSD 3 Month Hedging Cost) is the annualised return, in percent, of selling dollars three months forward for euros. It is negative when hedging costs a euro investor money, so add it to the dollar yield. It follows the forward points. On 13 November 2026, EURUSD at 1.1023 and 3-month points of 44.77 (EUR3M Curncy) came to about -1.6% a year, and the index stood at -1.59.

    Notes

    • FXHCUSEU is the other side: a dollar investor hedging euros earns about the same amount.
    • The hedge is rolled every three months. ust_hedged holds only while the gap between dollar and euro short rates stays where it is.
    • Holidays differ between New York, Frankfurt and the FX market. Month-ends hide that. For daily data, add the options from Three markets, one calendar.
    • To get the hedge cost known on each trade date, sort both frames by date and use trades.join_asof(hedge, on="date"). Each trade gets the last value on or before its date.

    Fiscal-year leverage¶

    YEARLY, periodicityAdjustment=FISCAL, currency

    Reported and adjusted EBITDA, net debt and net leverage for each fiscal year, for three companies whose years end in different months: IBM (December), Vodafone (March) and Microsoft (June). In USD billions.

    from datetime import date

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        ["IBM US Equity", "VOD LN Equity", "MSFT US Equity"],
        ["TRAIL_12M_EBITDA", "EBITDA_ADJUSTED", "NET_DEBT"],
        start_date=date(date.today().year - 4, 1, 1),
        end_date=date.today(),
        options={
            "periodicitySelection": "YEARLY",
            "periodicityAdjustment": "FISCAL",
            "currency": "USD",
        },
    )
with pl.Config(tbl_rows=20):
    print(
        df.select(
            company=pl.col("security").str.split(" ").list.first(),
            fiscal_year_end="date",
            ebitda=(pl.col("TRAIL_12M_EBITDA") / 1000).round(1),
            ebitda_adj=(pl.col("EBITDA_ADJUSTED") / 1000).round(1),
            net_debt=(pl.col("NET_DEBT") / 1000).round(1),
            leverage=(pl.col("NET_DEBT") / pl.col("EBITDA_ADJUSTED")).round(1),
        )
    )

    Watch out

    • "periodicityAdjustment": "FISCAL" puts every field on the company's fiscal year end. Without it, TRAIL_12M_EBITDA lands on the last trading day of each calendar year and NET_DEBT on the fiscal year end. When the two dates differ, as they do every year for Vodafone and Microsoft, the year has two rows, each half null.
    • CALENDAR does not calendarise the figures; it only relabels them. Microsoft's fiscal year to June 2023 and Vodafone's to March 2023 both come back dated 2022-12-30. Keep FISCAL and the real period end.
    • Vodafone reports in EUR and "currency": "USD" converts. EBITDA is converted at the average rate of the year and net debt at the rate at year end: about 1.16 and 1.11 USD per EUR for fiscal 2022. So leverage in USD can differ from leverage in the reporting currency.

    Notes

    • TRAIL_12M_EBITDA is EBITDA as reported. EBITDA_ADJUSTED takes out one-off items. Vodafone's fiscal 2023 shows the difference: 25.3 reported, 16.2 adjusted.
    • Amounts are in millions; the recipe divides by 1,000. Negative net debt is net cash.
    • For period offsets, segments, adjusted figures and estimates in BQL, see Company Fundamentals.

    Weekly candles¶

    PX_OPEN, PX_HIGH, PX_LOW, WEEKLY

    Weekly open, high, low and close of the S&P 500 for the last eight weeks, with the week's range and change in percent.

    from datetime import date, timedelta

import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bdh(
        "SPX Index",
        ["PX_OPEN", "PX_HIGH", "PX_LOW", "PX_LAST"],
        start_date=date.today() - timedelta(weeks=8),
        end_date=date.today(),
        options={"periodicitySelection": "WEEKLY"},
    )
print(
    df.select(
        week_ending="date",
        open="PX_OPEN",
        high="PX_HIGH",
        low="PX_LOW",
        close="PX_LAST",
        range_pct=(
            100 * (pl.col("PX_HIGH") - pl.col("PX_LOW")) / pl.col("PX_LAST")
        ).round(1),
        chg_pct=(100 * pl.col("PX_LAST").pct_change()).round(1),
    )
)

    Pitfalls¶

    What surprised us while writing these recipes.

    Symptom Cause Fix
    Weekly rows on Wednesdays "periodicityAdjustment": "ACTUAL" ends weeks on the weekday of end_date Leave it out, or CALENDAR, for Friday closes
    The current week or month is missing Weekly and monthly BDH return complete periods only Add a daily request, or use BQL with per=W
    Nulls after a pivot, holidays missing Each security keeps its own trading days NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS with PREVIOUS_VALUE
    "nonTradingDayFillMethod": "PREVIOUS_VALUE" changes nothing The default fill option adds no days Add "nonTradingDayFillOption": "NON_TRADING_WEEKDAYS" or ALL_CALENDAR_DAYS
    Two rows per year, each half null YEARLY without FISCAL: trailing fields on calendar year ends, reported fields on fiscal year ends "periodicityAdjustment": "FISCAL"
    Microsoft's fiscal 2023 dated 2022-12-30 CALENDAR relabels fiscal years FISCAL
    A column named BX218 Field ids are accepted, and the column keeps the name you asked for Use the mnemonic, INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP, or rename
    Index OAS 100 times smaller than in BQL INDEX_OAS_TSY is in percent INDEX_OAS_TSY_BP
    The CDS basis has the opposite sign to its description CDS_INDEX_INTRINSIC_BASIS_VALUE is quoted minus intrinsic Read positive as "index wider than its constituents"
    Today's intrinsic spread is null During the day only the quoted spread is there drop_nulls(), or end yesterday
    A CDS index jumps around 20 March or 20 September The generic ticker rolled to a new series Mark the roll dates, or use the series' own ticker
    FUND_FLOW is 0 while the fund's assets fell FUND_FLOW is for one share class, FUND_TOTAL_ASSETS for all of them Compare with FUND_CLASS_ASSETS, or estimate the fund's flows
    Leverage in USD differs from leverage in EUR EBITDA converts at the year's average rate, net debt at the year-end rate Compute leverage in the reporting currency
    The hedged yield is too high FXHCEUUS is negative when hedging costs money Add it to the yield; do not subtract it
