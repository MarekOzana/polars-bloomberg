    Bonds and Capital Structure¶

    Which bonds and loans does a company have, how do they rank, who owns the issuer, and how much debt sits at each level?

    Data on this page

    This page answers these questions for the three large Swedish banks, for a European high-yield issuer (Virgin Media O2) and for a group with several issuers (Heimstaden).

    Amounts are converted to EUR so that they add up across currencies. The outputs below are live data as of 2026-11-17 and will differ when you run the queries. Before you adapt a query, read the Pitfalls.

    Quick reference¶

    You want Use One row per
    All live bonds of an issuer filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True), ticker in [...]) bond
    Its loans filter(loansUniv(ACTIVE), ticker == '...') loan
    Where a bond ranks normalized_payment_rank() (comparable across issuers), payment_rank() (as issued), basel_iii_designation() (bank capital) bond
    Debt per rank, per issuer or per parent sum(group(amt_outstanding(currency=EUR), ...)) group
    The company that owns the issuer parent(type=DIRECT), type=ULTIMATE or type=CAST company
    The parent's fundamentals next to each bond value(<field>, parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE) bond

    The capital structure (CAST) parent is the top company of the capital structure you see in CAST <GO>. Unlike the ultimate parent, it stops below private-equity sponsors, owners that hold the company as an investment, and governments that guarantee less than half of the group's debt. Since May 2026 BQL's parent() returns it with type=CAST.

    parent(type=...) Returns
    DIRECT (default) the immediate parent company
    ULTIMATE the company that ultimately controls the issuer
    CAST the top of the capital structure in CAST <GO>

    Swedish bank capital stacks¶

    normalized_payment_rank(), sum(group(..., [ticker, #rank]))

    Covered bonds, senior, senior non-preferred, Tier 2 and AT1 outstanding for SEB, Handelsbanken and Swedbank, in EUR billions.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#rank = normalized_payment_rank().value;
    #amt = group(amt_outstanding(currency=EUR).value / 1B, [ticker, #rank]);)
get(sum(#amt) as #eur_bn)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['SEB', 'SHBASS', 'SWEDA']))
"""
layers = {
    "Secured": "1 Secured (covered)",
    "Sr Unsecured": "2 Senior preferred",
    "Senior Bail-In": "3 Senior non-preferred",
    "Subordinated Unsecured": "4 Tier 2",
    "Jr Subordinated Unsecured": "5 AT1",
}
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
print(
    df.with_columns(
        layer=pl.col("#RANK").replace(layers), eur_bn=pl.col("#eur_bn").round(1)
    )
    .pivot(on="TICKER", index="layer", values="eur_bn")
    .sort("layer")
)

    Watch out

    Swedish banks also issue structured notes and MTNs under the same ticker, so the senior preferred counts are large.

    Notes

    • normalized_payment_rank() maps each bond to one ladder, the same for all issuers. Senior Bail-In is senior non-preferred (MREL), Subordinated is Tier 2 and Jr Subordinated is AT1.
    • The group column comes back as #RANK (upper case) and the ticker as TICKER.
    • consolidateduplicates=True keeps one line per bond instead of one per listing.

    AT1 and Tier 2 call schedule¶

    basel_iii_designation(), nxt_call_dt

    How much AT1 and Tier 2 each bank can call per year, in EUR millions.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
get(ticker, basel_iii_designation, nxt_call_dt,
    amt_outstanding(currency=EUR).value / 1M as #eur_m)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['SEB', 'SHBASS', 'SWEDA'] and
           basel_iii_designation() in ['Additional Tier 1', 'Tier 2']))
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
tier = pl.col("basel_iii_designation").replace(
    {"Additional Tier 1": "AT1", "Tier 2": "T2"}
)
print(
    df.with_columns(
        bank_tier=pl.col("ticker") + " " + tier,
        call_year=pl.col("nxt_call_dt").dt.year(),
        eur_m=pl.col("#eur_m").round().cast(pl.Int64),
    )
    .sort("bank_tier")
    .pivot(on="bank_tier", index="call_year", values="eur_m", aggregate_function="sum")
    .sort("call_year")
)

    AT1 distance to trigger¶

    value(..., parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE), capital_type_coco_trigger_level()

    The USD AT1s of the Swedish banks (the bonds on the Dealer Axes page), each with its bank's CET1 ratio and the bond's trigger, in percent of risk-weighted assets.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#cet1 = value(bs_tier1_com_equity_ratio(), parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE);
    #trigger = capital_type_coco_trigger_level();)
get(name, nxt_call_dt, #cet1, #trigger)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['SEB', 'SHBASS', 'SWEDA'] and crncy() == 'USD' and
           basel_iii_designation() == 'Additional Tier 1'))
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine(on="ID")
print(
    df.select(
        "name",
        "nxt_call_dt",
        "PERIOD_END_DATE",
        "#cet1",
        "#trigger",
        buffer_pp=pl.col("#cet1") - pl.col("#trigger"),
    ).sort("name", "nxt_call_dt")
)

    Watch out

    The projected item adds SOURCE_ID, the parent's ID. Join on ID, or the rows of the two items do not match.

    Notes

    • value(..., parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE) reads the field from each bond's capital structure parent, here the listed bank. PERIOD_END_DATE is the reporting quarter of the CET1 ratio.
    • For CET1 against the full capital requirement, see the Bank CET1 Ratio vs Requirements example.

    Bonds and loans with leverage¶

    loansUniv(ACTIVE), ebitda(fpt=LTM)

    The whole debt stack of Virgin Media O2, bonds and term loans, by rank. Leverage is the debt up to and including each rank divided by last-twelve-months EBITDA.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

fields = """
get(name, maturity, normalized_payment_rank().value as #rank,
    amt_outstanding(currency=EUR).value / 1M as #eur_m)
"""
bonds = "for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True), ticker == 'VMED'))"
loans = "for(filter(loansUniv(ACTIVE), ticker == 'VMED' and amt_outstanding() > 0))"
parent = """
get(name, ebitda(fpt=LTM, currency=EUR) / 1M as #ebitda)
for(parent('ZO388622 Corp', type=CAST))
"""
seniority = {"1st Lien Secured": 1, "Secured": 2, "Sr Unsecured": 3}
with BQuery() as bq:
    debt = pl.concat(
        [
            bq.bql(fields + bonds).combine().with_columns(kind=pl.lit("bond")),
            bq.bql(fields + loans).combine().with_columns(kind=pl.lit("loan")),
        ]
    )
    group = bq.bql(parent).combine()
ebitda = group["#ebitda"].item()
print(
    f"{group['name'].item()}: LTM EBITDA EUR {ebitda:,.0f}m ({group['PERIOD_END_DATE'].item()})"
)
print(
    debt.group_by("#rank")
    .agg(
        bonds=(pl.col("kind") == "bond").sum(),
        loans=(pl.col("kind") == "loan").sum(),
        eur_m=pl.col("#eur_m").sum().round().cast(pl.Int64),
    )
    .sort(pl.col("#rank").replace_strict(seniority, default=9))
    .with_columns(cum_leverage=(pl.col("eur_m").cum_sum() / ebitda).round(1))
)

    Output

    Watch out

    • Put the currency on the item, ebitda(..., currency=EUR), or use with(currency=EUR). with(ccy=EUR) is ignored without an error, and the numbers stay in the reporting currency. Check the CURRENCY column.
    • The order comes from Bloomberg's normalized rank. Read it as a debt stack, not a legal waterfall: debt of neighbouring ranks can share the same collateral.

    Notes

    • parent('ZO388622 Corp', type=CAST) turns one bond into its group, Vmed O2 UK Ltd, whose accounts cover all the issuing entities.
    • Private groups report less often; PERIOD_END_DATE shows which accounts you got.
    • loans() on the parent returns nothing for this group, so the loans come from loansUniv(ACTIVE) by ticker. amt_outstanding() > 0 drops repaid and undrawn facilities.
    • Some VMED bonds are vendor-financing notes issued by separate vehicles. Use issuer() to see the issuing entity of each bond.

    Direct, ultimate and CAST parent¶

    parent(type=DIRECT), ULTIMATE, CAST

    Heimstaden has two bond issuers: Heimstaden AB and its residential subsidiary Heimstaden Bostad. The three parent types answer "who owns this?" differently.

    from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
get(name, issuer(),
    value(name, parent(type=DIRECT), mapby=LINEAGE) as #direct,
    value(name, parent(type=ULTIMATE), mapby=LINEAGE) as #ultimate,
    value(name, parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE) as #cast)
for(['BJ158441 Corp', 'BR871064 Corp'])
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine(on="ID")
print(df.select("ID", "name", "issuer()", "#direct", "#ultimate", "#cast"))

    Notes

    • For the Heimstaden AB bond the ultimate parent is its owner, Fredensborg. The CAST parent stays at Heimstaden AB, the top of the capital structure.
    • For the Heimstaden Bostad bond the CAST parent is Heimstaden AB, which consolidates Bostad. Both issuers belong to one capital structure.
    • for(parent('BJ158441 Corp', type=CAST)) makes the parent the universe, so you can ask for its fundamentals directly.

    One capital structure, several issuers¶

    group(..., [#cast, ticker, #rank])

    All Heimstaden bonds grouped by CAST parent, issuer ticker and rank, in EUR millions.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#cast = value(name, parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE);
    #rank = normalized_payment_rank().value;
    #amt = group(amt_outstanding(currency=EUR).value / 1M, [#cast, ticker, #rank]);)
get(sum(#amt) as #eur_m, count(#amt) as #bonds)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['HEIBOS', 'HEIMST', 'HEMVLR']))
preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
print(
    df.select(
        "#CAST",
        "TICKER",
        "#RANK",
        "#bonds",
        eur_m=pl.col("#eur_m").round().cast(pl.Int64),
    ).sort("TICKER", "#RANK")
)

    Watch out

    • bonds('<company>') returns the bonds of that company only, not of its subsidiaries, so start from the tickers and let #cast show what belongs together.
    • dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'] matters: a group with one bond carries ORIG_IDS in count() but not in sum(), and the two items would not join.

    Notes

    • Jr Subordinated here are hybrid bonds, which rank below all senior debt of the same issuer. Heimstaden AB's debt is also structurally below Bostad's, because Heimstaden AB is a holding company.

    Largest capital structures in a high-yield index¶

    sum(group(..., #cast)) over members()

    The ten CAST parents with the most debt in the Bloomberg Pan-European High Yield index, with their share of the index.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#cast = value(name, parent(type=CAST), mapby=LINEAGE);
    #amt = group(amt_outstanding(currency=EUR).value / 1B, #cast);)
get(sum(#amt) as #eur_bn, count(#amt) as #bonds)
for(members('LP01TREU Index'))
preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine(on="ID")
print(
    df.select(
        "#CAST",
        "#bonds",
        eur_bn=pl.col("#eur_bn").round(1),
        share_pct=(100 * pl.col("#eur_bn") / pl.col("#eur_bn").sum()).round(1),
    )
    .filter(pl.col("#CAST").is_not_null())
    .sort("eur_bn", descending=True)
    .head(10)
)

    Pitfalls¶

    What surprised us while writing these recipes.

    Symptom Cause Fix
    A bond appears on two rows after combine() mapby=LINEAGE adds SOURCE_ID, which differs between items combine(on="ID"), or preferences(dropCols=['SOURCE_ID'])
    Thousands of rows from a parent universe A security without a parent returns the ID NULL_ID, a string that matches itself in every join Drop NULL_ID rows before combining, or keep one item
    Fundamentals in GBP or SEK although you asked for EUR with(ccy=EUR) leaves them in the reporting currency, without an error with(currency=EUR) or field(currency=EUR); check CURRENCY
    bonds('<parent>') misses the subsidiaries' bonds bonds() does not follow the CAST group: Heimstaden AB's list has no Heimstaden Bostad bonds Start from tickers or an index and group by parent(type=CAST)
    Screening all bonds of a country by CAST parent times out The parent is looked up for every bond in the universe Narrow the universe first (tickers, an index), then group
    Grouped items do not line up One-bond groups carry ORIG_IDS in some items only preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
