    Spread Statistics¶

    How wide are spreads for each rating? How much more does an AT1 pay than a bond with the same rating? Is the market wider than last month? And what spread does a bond have at the price you are about to pay?

    BQL answers the first three for a whole index in one request: it groups the bonds' spreads and sends back only the statistics. The last one is the YAS calculator (YAS <GO>), which BQL runs at any price and workout you give it.

    Data on this page

    Spreads are in basis points, yields in percent. The outputs below are live data as of 2026-11-17 and will differ when you run the queries. Before you adapt a query, read the Pitfalls.

    Quick reference¶

    You want Use One row per
    Count, median and quartiles of spreads per rating count(), median(), quantile(..., 0.75) of group(spread(...), by=[bb_composite(), ...]) group
    The same statistics month by month spread(dates=range(-6M, 0D), per=M), grouped by year() and month() of its DATE group and month
    One number per day for all bonds of an index avg(group(#s, by=#s().DATE)) day
    An index's own yield and spread yield(yield_type=YTW, universe_type=PROJECTED), spread(spread_type=OAS_SOVEREIGN, universe_type=RETURNS) date
    Spread or yield at your price, to call, maturity or a date spread(price=..., workout=TO_NEXT_CALL), TO_MATURITY, TO_WORST, TO_CUSTOM bond
    Discount margin of a floating rate note discount_margin(price=...) bond
    spread_type= Spread Works for
    Z (the default) Z-spread: constant spread over the swap zero curve bonds, indices
    OAS option-adjusted spread bonds
    G, I, ASW over government yields, over swap rates, asset swap bonds
    OAS_SOVEREIGN, OAS_SWAP index OAS over government or swap curve indices

    Name the spread type

    spread() without spread_type is the Z-spread, not the OAS, so every recipe names the type.

    OAS by rating: AT1 vs other bonds¶

    median(), quantile(), group(..., by=[bb_composite(), ...])

    Count, median and upper quartile OAS per rating from BBB+ to BB-, for AT1s (CoCos) and for all other bonds of three European indices: Bloomberg Contingent Capital Western Europe (I31095), Pan-European High Yield (LP01STAT) and Pan-European Aggregate Corporate (LP05STAT).

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#is_coco = if(capital_contingent_security(), true, false);
    #oas = group(spread(spread_type=OAS), by=[bb_composite(), #is_coco]);)
get(count(#oas) as #n, median(#oas) as #median, quantile(#oas, 0.75) as #q3)
for(filter(members(['I31095 Index', 'LP01STAT Index', 'LP05STAT Index']),
           bb_composite() in ['BBB+', 'BBB', 'BBB-', 'BB+', 'BB', 'BB-']))
preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
"""
ratings = pl.Enum(["BBB+", "BBB", "BBB-", "BB+", "BB", "BB-"])
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
stats = df.select(
    rating=pl.col("BB_COMPOSITE()").cast(ratings),
    at1="#IS_COCO",
    n="#n",
    median=pl.col("#median").round().cast(pl.Int64),
    q3=pl.col("#q3").round().cast(pl.Int64),
)
print(
    stats.filter(~pl.col("at1"))
    .drop("at1")
    .join(stats.filter(pl.col("at1")).drop("at1"), on="rating", suffix="_at1")
    .with_columns(premium=pl.col("median_at1") - pl.col("median"))
    .sort("rating")
)

    Notes

    • bb_composite() is today's rating. For a box plot, add quantile(#oas, 0.25) for the box and quantile(#oas, 0.05), quantile(#oas, 0.95) for the whiskers.
    • For the AT1 premium per rating over ten years, with charts, see the AT1 vs Credits OAS per Rating example.

    Median OAS by rating, month by month¶

    per=M, month(), year()

    Median OAS at the last six month-ends and today, for the ratings around the line between investment grade and high yield, in the Pan-European High Yield and Aggregate Corporate indices. crossover is BB+ minus BBB-: the extra spread for one notch below investment grade.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#oas = spread(spread_type=OAS, dates=range(-6M, 0D), per=M, fill=PREV);
    #year = year(#oas().DATE);
    #month = month(#oas().DATE);
    #median = median(group(#oas, by=[bb_composite(), #year, #month]));)
get(#median)
for(filter(members(['LP01STAT Index', 'LP05STAT Index']),
           bb_composite() in ['BBB', 'BBB-', 'BB+', 'BB']))
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
print(
    df.drop_nulls("DATE")
    .pivot(on="BB_COMPOSITE()", index="DATE", values="#median")
    .sort("DATE")
    .select(
        "DATE",
        pl.col("BBB", "BBB-", "BB+", "BB").round(),
        crossover=(pl.col("BB+") - pl.col("BBB-")).round(),
    )
)

    Watch out

    • per=M gives the last business day of each month and the latest close. frq=M instead steps back from today in whole months: run it on the 17th and every row is the 17th.
    • members() is today's index. Bonds that have left the index are missing from the history, and new issues count only from their issue date. Add count(group(...)) to see how many bonds each median uses.

    Notes

    • year() and month() of #oas().DATE put the period into the group key, so each rating gets one median per month.
    • A bond without any spread history falls into a group whose date is null. drop_nulls("DATE") removes that row.
    • fill=PREV carries the last spread forward over days without a price.
    • For AT1 vs other bonds per rating since 2014, see the AT1 vs Credits OAS per Rating example.

    Daily average Z-spread of index members¶

    avg(group(#s, by=#s().DATE))

    The average and median Z-spread of the bonds in the Bloomberg European Banks CoCo Tier 1 index (I31415), for each business day of the last two weeks, with the daily change of the average.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#z = spread(spread_type=Z, dates=range(-14D, 0D));
    #day = #z().DATE;
    #avg = avg(group(#z, by=#day));
    #median = median(group(#z, by=#day));
    #n = count(group(#z, by=#day));)
get(#avg, #median, #n)
for(members('I31415 Index'))
preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
print(
    df.filter(pl.col("#n") > 0)
    .sort("DATE")
    .select(
        "DATE",
        bonds="#n",
        avg=pl.col("#avg").round(1),
        median=pl.col("#median").round(1),
        change=pl.col("#avg").diff().round(1),
    )
)

    • bonds is the number of bonds with a spread that day. Weekends, and today until the close is stored, have none and are dropped. On a holiday in one market fewer bonds are priced, and the average moves because the set of bonds changed; check bonds before you read change as a market move.

    Notes

    • #z().DATE is the date of each spread. Grouping by it gives one value per day for all bonds together.
    • This is an equal-weighted average. The index's own spread is weighted by market value; the next recipe gets it.

    Index yield and spread, week by week¶

    universe_type, per=W

    Yield to worst and OAS of the Bloomberg Contingent Capital EUR index (I30904EU) at each Friday close for the last eight weeks. rates is the yield minus the spread: what is left once the credit spread is taken out, mostly the government bond yield. spread_pct is the spread's share of the yield, in percent.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#ytw = yield(yield_type=YTW, universe_type=PROJECTED);
    #oas = spread(spread_type=OAS_SOVEREIGN, universe_type=RETURNS);)
get(#ytw, #oas)
for(['I30904EU Index'])
with(dates=range(-8W, 0D), per=W, fill=PREV)
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine()
oas_pct = pl.col("#oas") / 100
print(
    df.select(
        "DATE",
        ytw=pl.col("#ytw").round(2),
        oas_bp=pl.col("#oas").round(),
        rates=(pl.col("#ytw") - oas_pct).round(2),
        spread_pct=(100 * oas_pct / pl.col("#ytw")).round(),
    )
)

    Watch out

    • Index analytics need universe_type; without it, yield() and spread() return null. PROJECTED is the index as it will be after the next rebalancing, RETURNS the index fixed for this month's returns. Yield to worst accepts only PROJECTED; spreads accept both.
    • per=W gives Friday closes, like weekly BDH data, and the latest close as the last row. frq=W would step back seven days at a time from today.
    • spread(spread_type=OAS) is null for an index. Use OAS_SOVEREIGN, OAS_SWAP, or Z with universe_type.

    Notes

    • The same numbers come from BDH: bq.bdh(["I30904EU Index"], ["INDEX_YIELD_TO_WORST", "INDEX_OAS_TSY"], start, end). INDEX_OAS_TSY is the OAS with RETURNS, but in percent instead of basis points.
    • The hedged index H30904EU has the same yield and spread; only its returns differ.

    Tier 2: spread to call or to maturity¶

    YAS: spread(price=..., workout=...)

    The EUR Tier 2 bonds of SEB, Handelsbanken and Swedbank at today's price: Z-spread and yield if the bank calls the bond at the first call date, and if it does not. This is the YAS calculator run from BQL.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#px = px_last();
    #z_call = spread(spread_type=Z, price=#px, workout=TO_NEXT_CALL);
    #z_mat = spread(spread_type=Z, price=#px, workout=TO_MATURITY);
    #y_call = yield(price=#px, workout=TO_NEXT_CALL);
    #y_mat = yield(price=#px, workout=TO_MATURITY);)
get(name, nxt_call_dt, #px, #z_call, #z_mat, #y_call, #y_mat)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['SEB', 'SHBASS', 'SWEDA'] and crncy() == 'EUR' and
           basel_iii_designation() == 'Tier 2'))
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine(on="ID")
print(
    df.select(
        "name",
        call="nxt_call_dt",
        price=pl.col("#px").round(2),
        z_call=pl.col("#z_call").round(),
        z_mat=pl.col("#z_mat").round(),
        y_call=pl.col("#y_call").round(2),
        y_mat=pl.col("#y_mat").round(2),
        extension=(pl.col("#z_mat") - pl.col("#z_call")).round(),
    ).sort("call")
)

    Watch out

    • price= takes a number or an expression; price=#px runs YAS at px_last(). workout= without price= is rejected with "Incorrect parameters provided".
    • TO_WORST picks the workout with the lower yield, not the lower spread. A bond can be worst to call although its spread to maturity is lower.
    • Without price=, spread() uses Bloomberg's own pricing source, not px_last(). Pass the same price= to every value you compare.

    Notes

    • workout= is TO_WORST (the default), TO_NEXT_CALL, TO_NEXT_PUT, TO_MATURITY or TO_CUSTOM. yield() and the other spread types take the same parameters.
    • These bonds reset their coupon to the swap rate plus the original margin if they are not called. extension is the spread to maturity minus the spread to call. When it is positive, the reset margin is above today's market spread and the bank saves money by calling. When it is negative, an investor earns less spread if the bond is not called.

    One bond at four prices¶

    YAS: workout=TO_CUSTOM, workout_date

    The SEB 4 08/18/37 Tier 2 bond, first call 18 August 2032, at four prices: Z-spread to the call, to a custom date two years later, and to maturity, and the yield to worst.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

bond = "DO405623 Corp"  # SEB 4 08/18/37, first call 2032-08-18
rows = []
with BQuery() as bq:
    for price in [96, 98, 100, 102]:
        query = f"""
        get(spread(spread_type=Z, price={price}, workout=TO_NEXT_CALL) as #z_call,
            spread(spread_type=Z, price={price}, workout=TO_CUSTOM,
                   workout_date=2034-08-18) as #z_2034,
            spread(spread_type=Z, price={price}, workout=TO_MATURITY) as #z_mat,
            yield(price={price}, workout=TO_WORST) as #ytw)
        for(['{bond}'])
        """
        rows.append(bq.bql(query).combine().with_columns(price=pl.lit(price)))
print(
    pl.concat(rows).select(
        "price",
        z_call=pl.col("#z_call").round(),
        z_2034=pl.col("#z_2034").round(),
        z_mat=pl.col("#z_mat").round(),
        ytw=pl.col("#ytw").round(2),
    )
)

    • workout=TO_CUSTOM, workout_date=... assumes the bond is repaid at 100 on that date; workout_price= sets another redemption price. Use it for a call that comes late, or for the date you plan to sell.
    • The spread to the nearest date moves most with the price: the gain or loss against 100 is spread over fewer years.
    • The other direction works too: price(spread=100, spread_type=Z, workout=TO_NEXT_CALL) is the price for a Z-spread of 100 bp to the call, and price(yield=4.5, workout=TO_NEXT_CALL) the price for a yield.

    SEK AT1 floaters: discount margin¶

    YAS: discount_margin(price=...)

    The floating rate AT1s in SEK of six Swedish banks: the quoted margin over STIBOR, the discount margin at today's price, and the discount margin if you pay one point more.

    import polars as pl
from polars_bloomberg import BQuery

query = """
let(#px = px_last();)
get(name, nxt_call_dt, #px, flt_spread() as #margin,
    discount_margin(price=#px) as #dm,
    discount_margin(price=#px + 1) as #dm_plus_1)
for(filter(bondsUniv(ACTIVE, consolidateduplicates=True),
           ticker in ['SEB', 'SHBASS', 'SBAB', 'LFBANK', 'KLARNA', 'SVEABK'] and
           crncy() == 'SEK' and cpn_typ() == 'FLOATING' and
           basel_iii_designation() == 'Additional Tier 1'))
"""
with BQuery() as bq:
    df = bq.bql(query).combine(on="ID")
print(
    df.select(
        "name",
        call="nxt_call_dt",
        price=pl.col("#px").round(2),
        margin="#margin",
        dm=pl.col("#dm").round(),
        dm_plus_1=pl.col("#dm_plus_1").round(),
        bp_per_point=(pl.col("#dm") - pl.col("#dm_plus_1")).round(),
    ).sort("call")
)

    Notes

    • flt_spread() is the quoted margin, the spread the coupon pays over the reference rate. discount_margin(price=100) is close to it: at par a floater earns its margin.
    • For these perpetual notes the discount margin is to the next call (TO_WORST and TO_NEXT_CALL give the same value). workout=TO_MATURITY changes it.
    • bp_per_point is the discount margin you give up for each point of price. Close to the call date one point costs a lot of margin, so check the call date before you pay up for a short floater.
    • price=#px + 1 shows that price= takes arithmetic too.

    Pitfalls¶

    What surprised us while writing these recipes.

    Symptom Cause Fix
    "OAS" numbers match the Z-spread spread() without a type is the Z-spread spread(spread_type=OAS)
    Each group appears twice after combine(), half of the values null A null group key: capital_contingent_security() is null for most non-CoCos, and combine() does not join null keys if(capital_contingent_security(), true, false)
    Grouped items over dates do not join, three times as many rows ORIG_IDS is null in every row preferences(dropCols=['ORIG_IDS'])
    A monthly history on the 17th of each month frq=M steps back from the end date per=M for month-end closes
    A weekly history on Tuesdays frq=W steps back seven days from today per=W for Friday closes
    An index's yield or spread is null No universe_type universe_type=PROJECTED (the only one for yield to worst) or RETURNS
    "Incorrect parameters provided" for spread() or yield() workout= without price= Add price=px_last() or a number
    The index OAS is 100 times smaller in BDH INDEX_OAS_TSY is in percent, BQL in basis points Multiply the BDH value by 100
    The daily average jumps on a holiday Fewer bonds are priced that day Compare the bond count, or use the median
    Two aliases, one column BQL merges identical expressions, such as spread() and spread(spread_type=Z), into one item Ask for each expression once
